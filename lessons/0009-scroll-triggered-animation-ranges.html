Trigger Inside A Scroll Range

Skill: use animation-range to say which part of the view timeline should contain the reveal.

The Problem This Solves

Attach animation-timeline: view() to an element and by default the animation is stretched across the element's entire trip through the viewport — it starts when the top edge enters at the bottom of the screen and only finishes as the element leaves at the top. For a reveal, that's wrong in a specific, predictable way: the card spends its whole on-screen life mid-fade, hitting full opacity only as the user scrolls past it. The user never sees the finished state while actually looking at the element.

What you actually want for a reveal: animate briefly as the element arrives, then sit finished and stable while it's being read. animation-range is the tool that expresses this — it crops the timeline to just the slice of the journey you care about.

The Idea: Crop The Timeline

.reveal {
  animation: reveal linear both;
  animation-timeline: view();
  animation-range: cover 0% cover 20%;
}

Read the range as two waypoints in the element's viewport journey: start mapping at this point, finish at that point. cover means "the full span from first pixel entering to last pixel leaving," so cover 0% cover 20% says: run the whole animation during the first fifth of the trip. Outside the range, animation-fill-mode: both (from lesson 6) holds the edge keyframes — before the range you're at the start state, after it you stay at the finished state. That's what makes the card stable while it's read.

The range is also your feel dial: a wider range (cover 0% cover 40%) makes the reveal feel slower and more gradual; a later start (cover 20% cover 45%) makes the element wait until it's well inside the viewport before waking up. There are other keywords — entry covers just the entering phase, exit the leaving phase — but cover plus percentages handles most reveal work.

On Your Portfolio

This is exactly the pattern your viewport-reveal registry item uses, and as you identified earlier: the same fixed range can serve every item — each element gets its own view timeline automatically, so cover 0% cover 20% staggers naturally as items occupy different scroll positions. No per-item tuning needed until you want elements at the same scroll depth to differ; that's where per-sibling dynamic ranges come in, a future lesson.
